# Quadrotor Inner-Loop Control for Disturbance Compensation

**C++/ROS 2 implementations of three disturbance-compensating quadrotor control approaches: disturbance-estimating torque control, adaptive sliding-mode control (ASMC), and an Adaptive INDI controller experiment.**

This repository is part of the [Robust Goal-Directed Aerial Manipulation research project](https://github.com/1shit4/aerial-manipulation-rl-control). In aerial manipulation, motion of the attached arm produces additional forces and moments that can disturb the quadrotor and degrade end-effector tracking. The controllers here investigate lower-level flight control for tracking commanded vehicle motion in the presence of such disturbances. The repository focuses on **controller implementations and PX4–Gazebo tests**.

## Control Flow and PX4 Integration

### Standard PX4 control cascade

PX4 normally controls a multicopter using nested feedback loops:

```text
Position setpoint
    → Position controller → desired velocity
    → Velocity controller → desired acceleration
    → Acceleration/yaw conversion → desired attitude + collective thrust
    → Attitude controller → desired body rates (p, q, r)
    → Angular-rate controller → desired body torques
    → Control allocator → individual motor commands
    → ESC or simulated motor model → rotor speeds
```

Each loop compares its reference with measured or estimated vehicle motion. The inner angular-rate controller generates the roll, pitch, and yaw torques required to track the commanded body rates. PX4's control allocator combines these torques with collective thrust to produce **normalized motor actuation commands**, which are then translated into motor speeds by the actuator hardware or simulation model.

### Controllers implemented in this repository

Each controller replaces one or more inner stages of the PX4 cascade shown above. Desired references are generated by the preceding control stages, and feedback about the vehicle's motion is obtained from PX4.

| Controller | Input to controller | Output from controller | PX4 stage replaced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Disturbance-estimating control** | Desired attitude and body rates; measured attitude and angular rates | Body torque and collective thrust | Angular-rate control (rate-to-torque computation) |
| **Adaptive sliding-mode control (ASMC)** | Desired attitude, body rates, and angular acceleration; measured attitude and angular rates | Body torque and collective thrust | Angular-rate control (rate-to-torque computation) |
| **Adaptive INDI** | Desired and measured angular acceleration; collective-thrust command | Four individual normalized motor commands | Angular-rate control **and** control allocation |

For the first two controllers, PX4's **control allocator** converts the commanded torque and thrust into individual motor commands. **Adaptive INDI** computes the motor commands itself and therefore bypasses PX4's rate controller and control allocator. The ESC or simulated motor model then converts the motor commands to rotor speeds.

## Implemented Controllers

### 1. Disturbance-Estimating Torque Control

**Source:** [`src/il_control_system/`](src/il_control_system/) · [`il_control.hpp`](src/il_control_system/include/il_control_system/il_control.hpp)

This controller combines geometric attitude feedback with an estimate of the **lumped rotational disturbance**, including effects not captured by a nominal inertia model.

For current and desired orientations $R, R_d \in SO(3)$, and body rates $\omega,\omega_d$, the tracking errors are

```math
e_R = \frac{1}{2}(R_d^\top R-R^\top R_d)^\vee,
\qquad
e_\omega = \omega-R^\top R_d\omega_d.
```

A disturbance estimate is obtained from the difference between the applied torque and nominal inertial response:

```math
\hat H = \bar\tau - \bar J\,\overline{\dot\omega},
```

where $\bar J$ is the nominal inertia and the torque and angular-acceleration terms are filtered. The implementation **integrates the commanded torque over the interval between new gyroscope measurements**, so the torque and acceleration estimates correspond to the same measurement window even when control and sensor updates arrive at different rates.

The commanded body torque is

```math
\tau_c = \bar J\left(\dot\omega_d-K_R e_R-K_\omega e_\omega\right)+\hat H.
```

The result is torque-limited, converted to model-specific normalized PX4 torque setpoints, and published alongside collective thrust computed by the outer loop. The code also supports a PID-only reference mode.

### 2. Adaptive Sliding-Mode Control (ASMC)

**Source:** [`src/asmc_system/`](src/asmc_system/) · [`asmc.hpp`](src/asmc_system/include/asmc_system/asmc.hpp)

ASMC computes corrective torque from **attitude and angular-rate tracking errors**, with an error-dependent switching term to counter disturbances and uncertainty.

A sliding variable combines the attitude error and rate error:

```math
s=e_\omega+\Phi\odot e_R,
```

where $\odot$ indicates element-wise multiplication. The implemented torque law is

```math
\tau_c=-\Lambda\odot s
+\bar M\left(\dot\omega_d-\Phi\odot e_\omega\right)
-\rho\odot\mathrm{sat}(s/v),
```

with switching-gain vector

```math
\rho=k_0+k_1\odot|e_R|.
```

The gain terms are updated from the sliding-error magnitude and bounded in the C++ implementation. A saturation function is used instead of a discontinuous sign function near the sliding surface. The resulting body-torque setpoints and the collective thrust computed by the outer loop are passed to PX4 for control allocation.

This package provides both **ASMC and a PID/body-rate reference path**.

### 3. Adaptive Incremental Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion (Adaptive INDI)

**Source:** [`src/adaptive_indi_sytem/`](src/adaptive_indi_sytem/) · [`indi_proper.hpp`](src/adaptive_indi_sytem/include/adaptive_indi_system/indi_proper.hpp) · [`controllers.hpp`](src/adaptive_indi_sytem/include/adaptive_indi_system/controllers.hpp)

Adaptive INDI uses **angular-acceleration feedback** to correct the commanded motor actuation and reject disturbances affecting rotational motion. It computes the correction for each motor, instead of outputting a body-torque setpoint for PX4's control allocator.

The nominal model relates squared rotor speeds to angular acceleration:

```math
\dot\omega=G\,\omega_m^2,
\qquad G=J^{-1}B,
```

where $J$ is the vehicle inertia matrix and $B$ maps squared rotor speeds to body torques. A rate controller generates the desired angular acceleration $\alpha_d$, while filtered gyroscope measurements provide the measured angular acceleration $\alpha_m$. The INDI correction is

```math
\Delta\omega_m^2=G^+(\alpha_d-\alpha_m),
```

where $G^+$ is a regularized pseudoinverse of the control-effectiveness matrix. The motor-level correction is combined with the collective-thrust command:

```math
u_i=\mathrm{clip}\!\left(u_{\mathrm{collective}}+
\frac{\Delta\omega_{m,i}^2}{\omega_{\max}^2},0,1\right).
```

The **adaptive effectiveness formulation** extends INDI by estimating the control-effectiveness matrix from observed changes in angular acceleration and rotor-speed commands. With $\Delta\dot\omega_k$ denoting the measured change in angular acceleration and $\Delta\omega_{m,k}$ the rotor-speed increment, an LMS-style update takes the form

```math
\hat G_{k+1}^{*}=\hat G_k+
\eta\left(\Delta\dot\omega_k-\hat G_k\Delta\omega_{m,k}\right)
\Delta\omega_{m,k}^{\top},
```

followed by regularization toward the model-based effectiveness matrix:

```math
\hat G_{k+1}=(1-\lambda)\hat G_{k+1}^{*}+\lambda G_{\mathrm{model},k}.
```

Here $\eta$ is the adaptation gain and $\lambda$ controls the regularization toward the nominal model. This formulation also uses filtering, excitation checks, and bounds to limit poorly conditioned updates. The resulting motor commands follow PX4's direct actuator path, bypassing its rate controller and control allocator.

## Tracking and Disturbance Tests

The repository includes ROS 2 trajectory planners and configurations for evaluating vehicle tracking with different inner-loop controllers:

- **Hover and waypoint tracking:** position-setpoint generation with recorded commanded and measured vehicle positions.
- **Square trajectories:** waypoint sequences including a 4 m × 4 m square in the ASMC planner and separate YAML-defined square paths.
- **Circular trajectories:** YAML configurations containing 16 points around a **3 m-radius circle**, at a nominal height of **5 m** (PX4 NED `z = -5 m`).
- **Manipulator-induced disturbances:** the ASMC trajectory planner publishes periodic motion commands for two arm joints after takeoff. Its active configuration uses **0.1 Hz sinusoidal motion**, amplitudes of **1.0 rad** and **1.3 rad**, and a fixed third joint, providing a changing arm configuration during flight tracking.

The trajectory planner computes position-error statistics, and [`plot_trajectory.py`](plot_trajectory.py) supports visualization of reference/actual vehicle trajectories and manipulator-joint motion. The repository contains the implementations and test configurations, rather than a consolidated numerical controller benchmark.
